Summary: English proficiency levels & writing scales

1. Proficiency frameworks (describe a learner's level)

- CEFR: A1–A2 beginner, B1–B2 intermediate, C1–C2 advanced. Most widely used; IELTS, TOEFL and Cambridge exams map to it.
  https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages
- ACTFL (US): Novice → Intermediate → Advanced → Superior → Distinguished, with low/mid/high sub-levels.
  https://www.actfl.org/proficiency-guidelines-overview
- ILR (US govt): 0–5 with "+" steps.
  https://www.govtilr.org/Skills/ILRscale1.htm
- GSE (Pearson Global Scale of English): 10–90, finer-grained than CEFR; free learning objectives by level.
  https://www.pearson.com/languages/why-pearson/the-global-scale-of-english.html
- CLB (Canadian Language Benchmarks): 1–12.
  https://www.language.ca

2. Readability formulas (score a piece of text)

- Flesch Reading Ease (0–100), Flesch-Kincaid Grade, Gunning Fog, SMOG, Coleman-Liau, Dale-Chall, Lexile.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flesch%E2%80%93Kincaid_readability_tests

3. Controlled vocabularies (restrict allowed words)

- Basic English (~850 words), Globish (~1,500 words), Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100, for manuals).
  https://www.asd-ste100.org

4. Word-frequency lists

- Oxford 3000/5000 (tagged by CEFR level).
  https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/about/wordlists/oxford3000-5000
- Academic Word List (Coxhead), General Service List.

5. Plain language guidelines

- Federal Plain Language Guidelines (reposted copy):
  https://centerforplainlanguage.org/learning-training/tools-training/

Practical use:

- Ask for a level + standard, e.g. "B1, Indian spelling, friendly tone, under 100 words."
- CEFR is easiest to ask for; Flesch-Kincaid is easiest to measure; "Oxford 3000 words only" is a strict but natural-sounding constraint.
